# The Margins: editorial preferences

These are the owner's standing preferences for new articles and edits across The Margins, including article bodies, introductions, captions, source lists, Field Notes, project pages and homepage copy. Apply them when working on this repository. Later explicit user instructions take precedence.

## Reader-facing prose

- Write for the journal's readers. Do not carry the editing conversation or production instructions into published copy. Exclude wording such as "you told me," "based on the material you provided," "the author previously described," "as requested," or explanations of what the owner supplied to an assistant. Keep working notes out of articles, captions and bibliographies.
- Use direct, concrete, restrained English. Prefer specific observations, dates, actions and supported facts. Avoid generic moral lessons, stock calls to action, repeated rhetorical questions and paragraphs that merely explain the significance of the preceding paragraph.
- Personal introductions should preserve the author's first-person voice and warmth while remaining moderately formal and suitable for a journal. State how a piece began and any competition or award details plainly. Avoid both stiff archival language and overly casual phrasing such as "This is the version I submitted."
- Check repetition within each article and across the site. Do not repeat the same conclusion in an introduction, transition and ending. A paragraph should contribute information or analysis, not merely announce what the article will explain or conclude.
- Make the smallest edits that resolve a real problem. Preserve the author's voice and useful reflection. Do not keep rewriting acceptable prose just to make it seem less polished, and do not introduce errors or invented personal details.
- Do not claim that style proves authorship or promise a particular AI-detector result. The editorial goal is clear, natural, accurate writing.

## Sources, photographs and personal experience

- The numbered Sources lists should contain public sources with working public URLs. Do not add private emails, personal spreadsheets, author recollections or "materials provided by the author" as bibliography entries. If references change, update every corresponding number and retain the correct source-to-claim mapping.
- The owner explicitly permits personal photographs, email screenshots, Field Notes and accounts of personal experience. Do not remove them, people's names in those materials, or ordinary photo/video credits merely because they came from the owner. A normal credit such as "Photograph by Jerry Zou" is different from a note about the editing conversation.
- Preserve honest attribution and necessary limitations: direct experience, personal correspondence, public institutional accounts and other outlets' interviews must remain distinguishable. Never relabel private material as a public source or imply a direct interview that did not occur.
- Preserve scientific conditions, actual research ownership, the distinction between laboratory results and field deployment, and documented correction notices. Do not remove these simply because they sound careful or technical. Do not invent completed project work, outcomes or institutional endorsements.
