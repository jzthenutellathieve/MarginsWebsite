Housing Navigation

Notes from volunteer work with Church World Service, looking for rental housing in Long Beach.

A listing can look affordable before you find out how the landlord checks income. In my housing searches, I have had to look beyond the advertised rent to understand whether a family could apply. That is the part of housing navigation I want to record here.

What Housing Navigation Actually Looks Like

My work has included finding rental listings, calling landlords and organizing the answers for case managers. I record rent and bedroom count, along with deposits, income requirements, credit checks and whether housing vouchers are accepted. Those details help show which listings are worth following up.

One property I contacted used its market rent to calculate the income requirement, even though the advertised rent was lower. I recorded both figures so a case manager could see how the requirement was calculated. Other properties need to be checked individually.

What I want to keep track of

The next step is to make the housing notes easier to use and update: when a landlord last confirmed a requirement, what still needs checking, and what a case manager finds useful. I would like to share an anonymized example of that process here as the work develops.
